# Introduction

Pneumonia classification from chest radiographs is a useful healthcare computer-vision task because image interpretation requires recognizing subtle patterns in lung opacity. In this project, we study binary classification of normal and pneumonia-labelled pediatric chest X-rays from the public Kermany dataset [1]. The available distribution contains 5,216 training records, 16 validation records and 624 test records. Pneumonia-labelled images outnumber normal images, and the data originate from a single hospital. These properties make class-specific performance and the evaluation protocol important when interpreting a headline accuracy.

Transfer learning enables this task to use visual features learned from a larger image collection. Chouhan et al. [2] combined five ImageNet-pretrained convolutional neural networks with frozen early layers, trainable late blocks and classifiers, and majority voting. They reported 96.39% accuracy, 99.62% sensitivity and 99.34% AUC. More recently, Singh et al. [3] used pretrained DeiT-Base and reported 97.61% accuracy, F score 0.952 and AUC 0.966. Different evaluation protocols prevent a direct numerical ranking, and the transformer's split table has inconsistent class totals.

We select Chouhan et al. because its frozen early weights and five-model voting rule permit a bounded reconstruction with public data and standard implementations. We preserve our initial cached five-epoch experiment and the cached15-epoch audit. Our latest experiment uses five fixed epochs on all5232 paper-training records, full FP32 image forwards and fresh augmentation. Early weights remain frozen while BatchNorm running statistics adapt during training. Three models trained on the Mac and two in an isolated CPU VPS. The shorter schedule and unspecified author settings remain limitations. We report accuracy together with pneumonia sensitivity, normal recall, precision, both class F1 scores, macro F1 and ROC AUC. An always-pneumonia prediction can obtain62.5% test accuracy while never recognizing a normal image, illustrating why balanced evaluation matters.

Our proposed system combines lung-focused cropping with a CNN and Vision Transformer ensemble. A fixed pretrained anatomical segmenter supplies a lung bounding box with a10% margin. A pretrained small transformer complements the five CNNs, and validation-selected probability weights combine their outputs. Cropping may increase the input resolution devoted to lung tissue, while patch self-attention may contribute complementary predictions. These are two interventions: cropping is the primary M3 hypothesis, and adding the transformer is a separately stated extension. Four matched experiments compare full images and crops with CNN-only and CNN-plus-transformer classifiers.

Before implementation, we predict cropping increases macro F1 by0.01 to0.03 and specificity by2 to5 percentage points relative to a matched full-image CNN control. For the combined system relative to that control, we predict macro F1 +0.01 to +0.04 and accuracy0 to +3 percentage points, with pneumonia recall no more than2 points lower. These predictions are not measured results and need not be additive. Failed crops, overlapping model errors and limited pediatric data may eliminate any benefit. We make no clinical, global novelty or state-of-the-art claim. M2 establishes a working reconstruction, M3 records these hypotheses, and later milestones implement the matched comparisons and analyze failures.
